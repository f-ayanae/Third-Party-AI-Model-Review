## Facts extracted

- From 20 Nov 2026 s.37a BDSG applies (introduced by the CCD2 implementation act, BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 139) and s.31 BDSG is repealed; the release is based on the wording accessed on 19 Oct 2026.
- s.37a(2): scores may not use special-category data (Art 9(1) GDPR), age, gender, name, social network data, account inflows and outflows, or address data; no scores on minors; data must be demonstrably relevant under a scientifically recognised mathematical-statistical method and may not be used for other purposes.
- How far 'address data' extends remains unclear; whether a characteristic such as residence age falls under the ban is for supervisory authorities and courts to decide.
- s.37a(4): the creator of a score must, on request, disclose in clear and simple language the data and criteria used, the weighting of the most influential criteria, the meaning of the score, and the scores created and their recipients, within the Art 12(3) GDPR deadline; the information is retained for one year.
- s.37a(5): a person affected by a decision based on a score may contest it, state their view and have the decision made by a human.
- s.30(6) BDSG: for general consumer loans with automated creditworthiness assessment, the borrower may request human intervention.
- s.30(7) BDSG: a lender that rejects a contract because of credit-agency information must inform the consumer at once of that information, the database used and the data categories considered.
- s.37a(3) carries over the old s.31(2) rules on which unpaid claims may be counted.
- The consumer federation report of 18 Jun 2026 (L14) states that the bureau holds data on about 68 million people in Germany.
